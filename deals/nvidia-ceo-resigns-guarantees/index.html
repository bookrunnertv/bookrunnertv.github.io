Jensen Huang resigns: who covers Nvidia's $108.5 billion of promises?

13 November 2026 · watch time 6 min · Watch on YouTube

Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 11. But Nvidia doesn't just sell AI chips: it has promised to cover up to $108.5 billion if certain customers can't pay, holds $99 billion in equity investments and signed preliminary deals to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital. Who is actually on the hook now?

Nvidia's $108.5 billion promise

108.5 billion dollars. That is the most Nvidia has promised to cover if certain customers can't pay. It also holds 99 billion dollars in equity investments. And it signed up to help raise more than half a trillion. One trader told Reuters it has been called the "Bank of AI." On Wednesday, the CEO who led Nvidia through all of it resigned. So who is on the hook now?

“Easy. The new boss.”

Not quite. And the real answer changes how you read every headline about it.

What happened at Nvidia?

First, the facts from Nvidia's own filing. On November 11, the board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director. It took effect immediately. Nvidia gave no reason. Reports point to a board investigation. Colette Kress, the finance chief since 2013, is interim CEO and stays CFO. Harvey Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair, the company said. On Thursday afternoon the stock was down nearly 5 percent, Reuters reported.

“But why would lenders care who the CEO is?”

Because Nvidia doesn't just sell chips. It has made big financial promises, in three layers. These numbers come from its quarterly report, as of July 26, 2026.

Layer one: guarantees

Picture a friend renting an apartment. The landlord is nervous. So you sign too: if your friend stops paying, you pay part of the rent. That promise is a guarantee. You pay nothing unless something goes wrong.

“So it's free until it isn't.”

Exactly. Now make the apartment a giant AI data center in Pike County, Ohio. The landlord is an affiliate of SB Energy. The tenant is an affiliate of OpenAI. Nvidia guarantees defined parts of the lease and power payments, if the tenant defaults. The cap is 105 billion dollars. It was signed in August and grows as each of nine phases is built. The leases run for 20 years. It ends early if OpenAI reaches a satisfactory credit rating.

Add 3.5 billion for other AI clouds' data-center leases. Total maximum exposure: 108.5 billion. Nvidia's own debt is about 33 billion. The promises are more than three times that.

“And who did Nvidia promise this to? Huang?”

Hold that thought. First, two more layers. What you know now: Nvidia pays nothing unless a tenant stops paying. The cap is 108.5 billion.

Layers two and three

Layer two: purchase commitments. Money Nvidia has agreed to spend. 366 billion dollars in total. Most is supply and capacity: 279 billion, up from 119 billion a quarter earlier.

“It more than doubled in three months?”

Yes. Other items include 29 billion in cloud service deals. And Nvidia reports 36 billion committed to AI cloud partners, typically over six years. Layer three: stakes. Nvidia reports 99 billion dollars in equity investments. And 25 billion more already committed, in AI model makers, infrastructure financiers and other private companies. Then, in August, it signed preliminary deals with six big financial firms. Reuters named them: Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR.

The goal: more than 500 billion dollars of other people's money for AI infrastructure, over time. In August, Huang said Nvidia could backstop up to 125 billion of those deals, Reuters reported. What you know now: three layers. Guarantees, commitments, stakes. Plus a plan for half a trillion.

Who is on the hook?

Now, the question. Who do you think signed these promises? Not Jensen Huang personally. Nvidia, the company, did. A company's contracts don't leave with its CEO.

“So the 108 billion is still there?”

Yes. The signed guarantees and commitments are the company's, whoever runs it. What is less settled is the August plan. The filing itself says those preliminary arrangements may not lead to definitive agreements. That was already true before Wednesday. Now an interim CEO, and a board searching for a permanent one, will decide what gets signed.

“So the old promises stay. The new ones are an open question.”

Exactly. And the next clue arrives on Tuesday, November 17. Nvidia reports third-quarter results, as previously scheduled. Its next quarterly report should update these numbers.
