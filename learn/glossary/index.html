Glossary

The words behind the deals, in plain English. Each one links to the lesson or episode that explains it.

AT1 bond (Additional Tier 1)
A bank bond with a high coupon and no maturity date. If the bank needs rescuing, it can be written off or turned into shares; it sits just above the shares in the order of losses.Explained in: Credit Suisse: How $17 Billion of AT1 Bonds Were Wiped Out in One Weekend
Bank run
When many depositors try to take their money out of a bank at once. If enough leave together, the bank may have to sell what it owns at a loss.Explained in: Silicon Valley Bank: The $2.25bn Share Sale That Started a Run
Bond
One small piece of a big loan. Whoever holds it is a lender: it pays interest, and the full amount comes back on the end date.Explained in: What is a bond? Loans and bonds, explained with a lemonade stand
Bookbuilding
Collecting investors' orders first (how many shares, and the most they would pay), then setting one price for all and choosing who gets shares.Explained in: What is a bookrunner?
Bookrunner
The bank that runs the order book and prices the deal.Explained in: What is a bookrunner?
Bridge loan
Short-term money that holds a deal until long-term money arrives. Banks lend it expecting to replace it quickly with bonds sold to investors.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Collateral
Something the lender can take and sell if you don't pay.Explained in: What is a bond? Loans and bonds, explained with a lemonade stand
Coupon
The interest a bond pays, usually stated as a yearly percentage of its full amount.Explained in: Credit Suisse: How $17 Billion of AT1 Bonds Were Wiped Out in One Weekend
Debt
Borrowed money you must pay back, with interest.Explained in: Debt vs equity, explained with a lemonade stand
Equity
Money from owners, for a piece of the business. No duty to pay it back.Explained in: Debt vs equity, explained with a lemonade stand
Greenshoe (over-allotment option)
An option that lets the banks sell extra shares in an IPO, at the same price, when demand is strong. In Figma's IPO it was 15% of the base deal: about 5.5 million shares.Explained in: Figma IPO: $3.5 Billion Left on the Table, by Bloomberg's Count
Hung deal
Debt the banks promised to sell, and could not, so it stays on their own balance sheets.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Interest
The price you pay to use borrowed money.Explained in: Debt vs equity, explained with a lemonade stand
IPO (initial public offering)
A company's first sale of shares to the public.Explained in: What is a share? Stocks explained with a lemonade stand
Mark to market
Valuing a loan or a security at the price it would fetch today. A bank that means to sell a loan has to do it quarter after quarter.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Money left on the table
The first-day jump over the IPO price, times the shares sold: value that went to the buyers instead of the sellers. An on-paper measure.Explained in: Figma IPO: $3.5 Billion Left on the Table, by Bloomberg's Count
Negative goodwill
Buying a company for less than the fair value of its net assets. The gap is booked as profit.Explained in: Credit Suisse: How $17 Billion of AT1 Bonds Were Wiped Out in One Weekend
Oversubscribed
More shares ordered than are on offer. Figma's IPO book was reportedly about 40 times covered.Explained in: What is a bookrunner?
Profit
What's left after paying the costs.Explained in: Debt vs equity, explained with a lemonade stand
Roadshow
The sales tour where the banks pitch a deal to big investors, at a price range.Explained in: Figma IPO: $3.5 Billion Left on the Table, by Bloomberg's Count
Share
One equal piece of a company. Whoever holds it owns that piece.Explained in: What is a share? Stocks explained with a lemonade stand
Shareholder
Whoever owns shares: an owner of the company.Explained in: What is a share? Stocks explained with a lemonade stand
Sources and uses
The table of where a deal's money comes from and what it pays for: the honest version of the press release.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Stock market
A marketplace where anyone can buy and sell shares of the companies on it.Explained in: What is a share? Stocks explained with a lemonade stand
Syndicate
The group of banks sharing a deal's risk and fees.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Syndication
Selling most of a loan to investors within weeks of closing, and keeping the fees.Explained in: How Elon Musk's $44 Billion Twitter Deal Was Actually Financed
Underwriting
A promise to fund the deal even if no investor shows up.Explained in: What is a bookrunner?
Uninsured deposit
Money in a bank above the deposit-insurance limit. In the US, the FDIC insures each depositor up to $250,000.Explained in: Silicon Valley Bank: The $2.25bn Share Sale That Started a Run
Unrealized loss
The drop in what an asset, such as a bond, would fetch if sold today. It is real only if you sell.Explained in: Silicon Valley Bank: The $2.25bn Share Sale That Started a Run
Viability Event
In Credit Suisse's AT1 bonds, the regulator's judgment call that could trigger a write-down, including the case where the state promises extraordinary support that shores up the bank's capital.Explained in: Credit Suisse: How $17 Billion of AT1 Bonds Were Wiped Out in One Weekend
Write-down
Cutting what a bond is worth, here to zero: the holders' claim disappears.Explained in: Credit Suisse: How $17 Billion of AT1 Bonds Were Wiped Out in One Weekend
