What is a share? Stocks explained with a lemonade stand

4 November 2026 · watch time 5 min · Watch on YouTube

In 2025, the design company Figma put its shares on the stock market. Some of its shares came with one vote each. Others, with fifteen. So what is a share, really? Here it is, with a lemonade stand.

The takeaway

A share is one equal piece of a company, and whoever owns shares is an owner: a shareholder. A share's price is whatever a buyer and a seller agree on.

Step 1Cut into pieces

Remember Sunny's Lemonade? Max put in money for 40 percent of it. Sunny owns the other 60. To keep things simple, Sunny cuts the business into 100 equal pieces. Sunny keeps 60 pieces. Max gets 40.

Why bother? Pieces are easy to count, easy to split, and easy to sell. Each piece is called a share: one equal piece of a company. So if you own a share, you own a bit of the company? Exactly. Whoever owns shares is an owner: a shareholder.

Step 2What a share gives you

A share gives you three things.

  1. A slice of the profit, when the owners decide to pay it out. Every share gets the same amount. Sunny has more shares, so she gets more.
  2. A vote on big decisions, like who runs the business. One vote per share. So Sunny's 60 votes beat Max's 40.
  3. The right to sell: you can sell your shares to someone else.

Step 3What is a share worth?

Max wants to sell some of his shares to his friend Lily. They agree on a price: $300 per share. Max paid $250 a share back in Lesson 1, so he makes a profit on each one.

Who decides it's $300? Just the two of them. If both say yes, that's the price. Every share is the same, so if one sells for $300, each of the 100 is worth about that. Together: about $30,000.

That's a value on paper. Nobody paid $30,000, and Sunny got no cash from Lily's deal. The rule: a share's price is whatever a buyer and a seller agree on.

From friends to the stock market

Sunny's shares change hands between friends. Many big companies let anyone buy and sell their shares, on a stock market. You don't need to buy a whole company: even one share makes you an owner.

Millions of buyers and sellers, so the price moves all day long. A company's first sale of shares to the public has a name: an IPO.

Key terms

Share
One equal piece of a company. Whoever holds it owns that piece.
Shareholder
Whoever owns shares: an owner of the company.
Stock market
A marketplace where anyone can buy and sell shares of the companies on it.
IPO
A company's first sale of shares to the public.

The real thing: Figma

Back to Figma. Its shares started trading in New York in July 2025. But Figma has two kinds of shares: Class A shares get one vote each, Class B shares get fifteen.

The public bought Class A shares: one vote each. The founder, Dylan Field, controls Class B shares: fifteen votes each. So with well under half of the shares, he kept most of the votes.

That didn't stop buyers. They wanted far more Figma shares than were for sale. A share always means you own a piece. But not always the same say.

The full story, from the banks' side: Figma IPO: $3.5 Billion Left on the Table, by Bloomberg's Count

What you know now

  1. A share is one equal piece of a company.
  2. It can bring profit, a vote, and you can sell it.
  3. Its price is whatever buyers and sellers agree on.
